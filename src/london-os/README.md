# Ordnance Survey London variant

Route: `/london-os.html`. A continuous Three.js London world and top-floor study, with a charcoal/chalk interface informed by [Autopilot Careers](https://careers.joinautopilot.com/). “The Study” is the placeholder name. DM Sans is bundled with its [SIL Open Font Licence](./fonts/OFL.txt); headings use the local Georgia/Times serif stack. No reference artwork or models are reused.

The route reuses the existing garden-square geometry with optional flat-ground, uniform-roofline, pinboard-wall, Cornwall Gardens, palette and furnishing settings. Their defaults preserve `/gardens.html`. The entire London garden-square vignette, including its terraces, room and furnishings, is at 63% of its original scale: the earlier 20% and 25% reductions followed by a 5% increase. The Cornwall Gardens variant has white façades, two buildings on each east/west side, and no benches or fountain. Its garden, paths, fences and side paving are shortened by one 4.44-unit building bay, keeping the rear boundary fixed; trees and lamps follow the shorter layout. Nine smaller neighbouring houses, including three behind the study, fill gaps outside the square using the same plain pastel blocks and flat grey roof caps as the rest of London, with no windows, doors, chimneys or trim. The square's interior and room approach remain open. Camera targets and object anchors follow the shared group transform. The main portfolio and other routes retain their existing content and behavior.

## Geography and licence

The bundled [`geography.json`](./geography.json) contains 679 named public parks/gardens from [OS Open Greenspace](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/products/os-open-greenspace), 50 tidal-water features and three inland-water features from [OS OpenMap Local](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/products/os-open-map-local). These are genuine TQ shapefile polygons, not traced or substituted approximations. The JSON records source IDs/layers, anonymous download endpoints, archive checksums, retrieval date and processing parameters.

The Serpentine, Long Water and Round Pond use the reviewed `SurfaceWater_Area` IDs listed in the extractor. That layer has no name field, so these three names are editorial location labels, not OS-supplied names. The extractor fails if those IDs disappear. Trees are excluded from water polygons. Green parks/trees, blue water and varied pastel buildings belong to the map; the interface remains charcoal/chalk.

Contains Ordnance Survey data © Crown copyright and database right 2026. Reused under the [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/). Attribution is available through the page’s “OS geography & credits” control.

Coordinates remain British National Grid, **EPSG:27700**. The 26 × 20 km clip is `[516000, 170000, 542000, 190000]` metres, over five times the original prototype’s area. Rendering maps eastings to x and negative northings to z at 100 metres per scene unit. Heights and object scales are independently exaggerated.

The offline conversion simplifies rings at up to 5 metres, triangulates polygons with holes, checks triangulated area against ring area, clips each triangle to the rectangle, then quantises vertices to 0.1 metres. Simplification is reduced when that area check fails; refinements are explicitly recorded. Clipping triangles avoids joining disconnected polygon fragments across the clip boundary. The bundled result has 13,027 triangles and is approximately 513 KB before compression. Rendering merges it into two meshes.

## Reproduce the bundle

Run from the repository root with Node.js, Python 3, `curl` and `unzip`. `npm ci` restores the locked build dependencies, including the Three.js triangulator. No GIS service or API key is required.

```sh
npm ci
mkdir -p .cache/london-os/greenspace .cache/london-os/water
curl -fL 'https://api.os.uk/downloads/v1/products/OpenGreenspace/downloads?area=TQ&format=ESRI%C2%AE+Shapefile&redirect' -o .cache/london-os/greenspace.zip
curl -fL 'https://api.os.uk/downloads/v1/products/OpenMapLocal/downloads?area=TQ&format=ESRI%C2%AE+Shapefile&redirect' -o .cache/london-os/openmap.zip
unzip -jo .cache/london-os/greenspace.zip '*GreenspaceSite.*' -d .cache/london-os/greenspace
unzip -jo .cache/london-os/openmap.zip '*TidalWater.*' -d .cache/london-os/water
unzip -jo .cache/london-os/openmap.zip '*SurfaceWater_Area.*' -d .cache/london-os/water
curl -fL 'https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GeospatialPython/pyshp/2.3.1/shapefile.py' -o .cache/london-os/shapefile.py
PYTHONPATH=.cache/london-os python3 scripts/london-os-extract.py .cache/london-os > .cache/london-os/raw.json
node scripts/london-os-bundle.mjs
npm run build
npx playwright test --config scripts/london-os-playwright.config.js
```

The Python reader is unmodified, MIT-licensed [pyshp 2.3.1](https://github.com/GeospatialPython/pyshp/tree/2.3.1), used offline and not shipped. CP1252 decodes the source DBF names. Raw archives total approximately 225 MB and are not committed. Remove the specific `.cache/london-os` directory after processing.

The official download URLs track the current edition. The bundler verifies the original archive MD5 values recorded in its source before writing output. If OS replaces an archive, preserve the old zip for exact reproduction, or review the new [Greenspace metadata](https://api.os.uk/downloads/v1/products/OpenGreenspace/downloads?area=TQ&format=ESRI%C2%AE%20Shapefile) and [OpenMap Local metadata](https://api.os.uk/downloads/v1/products/OpenMapLocal/downloads?area=TQ&format=ESRI%C2%AE%20Shapefile), then deliberately update checksums and the retrieval date. Do not bypass the check. SHA-256 values for both original archives are also recorded in the bundle.

## Interaction and limitations

Drag or touch-drag to orbit; scroll or pinch to zoom. Buttons provide keyboard-accessible rotation, zoom and reset. One Explore button flies into the centred office. Its computer opens CV, shelves open Projects/Reading, the wall pinboard opens Work history and the cupboard opens Interests. Five individual lanyards/access cards reuse the original playful company illustrations. Each camera move stays in the same scene. Books open and passes turn over; the original readable content appears alongside them. Back, Escape, browser history and deep links work at every level. Reduced motion skips camera flights.

The Interests cabinet stands against the right wall with its front facing left into the room; its anchor and camera approach follow that orientation. The pinboard, frame and five lanyards are uniformly 28% smaller than the preceding version. Work history now approaches the board almost head-on at a close, device-specific distance, while selecting an individual pass still lifts and turns it in the room. Its top-right label is simply "Pinboard", without a duplicate panel subtitle. The masthead omits the location line. Reading buttons include title and author; the duplicate shelf headings/lists are omitted from this panel, while individual book links and the accessible source portfolio are retained.

The computer reuses the main route's terminal directly on its monitor, not in a side card. Three.js CSS3DRenderer matches the live HTML input to the monitor's world position, orientation and dimensions, including when orbiting. Its close camera approach keeps the `justinattw$` prompt, blinking block cursor and clickable `\ for commands` legible. Command help exposes `download resume.pdf`, `open resume.pdf` (new tab), `cat education.txt` and `exit` (back to the study). Commands can be typed and submitted or chosen to paste and run. The help button remains available after commands; Escape closes command help before leaving the computer. Education is read from the original portfolio, and the static CV remains available in the no-WebGL/no-JavaScript fallbacks. Other object cards fit their contents up to a viewport limit instead of stretching to the bottom; mobile camera framing updates when card height changes.

The study has a smaller rug, two rectangular white-framed windows with genuinely open wall apertures and lightly tinted transparent glazing, and no leftover roof fragment. Green wallpaper continues along both full side walls, with white cornices and skirting (the later green-wall request supersedes the earlier white-wall direction). A monstera and bamboo reuse the original plant models and disappear in object close-ups. The all-black cat reuses the original articulated model with a room-specific route: it grooms, drinks from its raised bowl, walks, jumps onto the chair and desk, paws at the keyboard and bats the monstera, which ruffles in response. Cat motion pauses in close-ups, in hidden tabs and for reduced motion. The compact London title panel is wider, with smaller type and no study invitation.

Sidecar Back controls live inside their panels, including Back to shelf/passes for selected items. Work history lists each company once in its picker; the selected company's name appears once as the panel heading. Project and reading shelf approaches are substantially closer and reading is centred between its two book rows. Object markers are hidden during close-ups so they cannot cover the monitor input.

Controls follow actual camera-to-room distance, including manual wheel, pinch and button zoom. Between 16 and 24 scene units, the five markers converge on Explore and crossfade; outside that range only the appropriate control set is interactive. Zooming away closes an object’s reading panel without forcing another camera flight. Automatic city/office changes replace the hash rather than adding history entries.

The content plugin injects the original `index.html` portfolio sections before the existing project and reading transforms. It fails the build if that source contract changes, rather than silently shipping empty content. `View contents`, a skip link, WebGL-failure handling and a JavaScript-disabled document keep the content accessible without navigating to the original room.

The scene renders on demand outside the study, with animation frames during flights, object motion and the visible cat's routine. All office furnishings are created before the first frame, so zooming in manually reveals the same populated room. Geography, fonts and portfolio content are bundled locally: no runtime external requests, map SDKs, accounts or click-throughs to another portfolio study. Outbound links and the CV download remain explicit visitor actions.

Only the park and water footprints are OS geography. City blocks, street-like gaps, trees and all heights are illustrative. Royal Albert Hall, Big Ben, the London Eye, Buckingham Palace, [Kensington Palace](https://www.ukcitymap.com/kensington-palace-greater-london-gps-location-coordinates.html), [Albert Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Bridge,_London), Nelson's Column and Marble Arch are original toy silhouettes, not surveyed building models. Kensington Palace remains a single block without the previously modelled side wings, now with a hipped slate roof, capped chimneys, side windows and a detailed west entrance with sash windows and steps. Buckingham Palace has a courtyard, detailed principal façade, balcony, roofline and forecourt railings; its garden front adds sash windows, a projecting central bay, columned porch, pediment and terrace steps. These rear elevations are artistic interpretations, not measured architectural reconstructions. The Eye's supports rotate with its wheel. Marble Arch has three genuinely open passages; Nelson's Column includes a pedestal, capital, statue and four small lion silhouettes. Background buildings and trees avoid landmark clearances. The bridge’s pink/cream towers and cables are deliberately exaggerated. The enlarged terrace and open-roof workspace are roughly in South Kensington, not at a precise address. Its garden square is part of that illustrative vignette, not an additional OS greenspace polygon.

Bearings use mean-latitude longitude scaling and an approximate 1.43-degree correction from true north to BNG grid north. The measured palace footprint normal is approximately 72.5 degrees; the bridge runs north-northwest to south-southeast (161.3 degrees southbound). Only rotations are derived from this data, not building geometry. [OpenStreetMap attribution and licence](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) are linked in the credits.

The same calculation gives [Marble Arch's central passage](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/667510421) a southbound bearing of 166.3 degrees. Approximate landmark locations remain unchanged except for the new monuments, positioned from Historic England's listed-building records: [Nelson's Column](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1276052), TQ 30017 80419 (`530017, 180419`), and [Marble Arch](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1239534), TQ 27852 80968 (`527852, 180968`). The palace models retain their illustrative locations, so their visual facings now use explicit map targets rather than applying footprint bearings at displaced anchors: Kensington's east entrance points towards the bundled Round Pond's area-weighted centre, BNG `(526181.1, 180113.2)` (approximately 95.5 degrees). Tests check the actual entrance/balcony vectors, not only the groups' local axes.

The eastern skyline adds original, exaggerated models of the Shard (split glass spire), Walkie Talkie (flared tower and roof garden), Gherkin (rounded taper and diagonal lattice), and nine Canary Wharf high-rises centred on a pyramid-topped One Canada Square. Approximate locations were estimated offline from the published coordinates for [the Shard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shard) (`51.5045, -0.0865`), [20 Fenchurch Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20_Fenchurch_Street) (`51.51128, -0.08353`), [30 St Mary Axe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_St_Mary_Axe) (`51.51444, -0.08028`) and [One Canada Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Canada_Square) (`51.50500, -0.019611`). A local tangent-plane estimate from the existing Big Ben anchor, with approximate grid convergence, was rounded to 50 metres: BNG `(532950, 180150)`, `(533100, 180900)`, `(533350, 181250)` and `(537550, 180300)` respectively. This is illustrative placement, not a surveyed coordinate conversion. The other Canary Wharf towers are an artistic cluster, not individual surveyed buildings. No source text, imagery or third-party models are bundled.

TidalWater omits some under-bridge areas; those source gaps remain visible. Only the three reviewed inland lakes are included. The selected greenspace class excludes private gardens and other land-use classes. Buildings are not a real street network, and their grid is not a substitute for OS building data. The garden-square terraces share a consistent roofline; background blocks still vary illustratively. Distance fog hides the world’s horizon rather than exposing a tabletop edge.
