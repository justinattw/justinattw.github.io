# Ordnance Survey London homepage

Route: `/`. A continuous Three.js London world and top-floor study, with a charcoal/chalk interface informed by [Autopilot Careers](https://careers.joinautopilot.com/). The header label is “Portfolio”. DM Sans is bundled with its [SIL Open Font Licence](./fonts/OFL.txt); headings use the local Georgia/Times serif stack. No reference artwork or models are reused. The original standalone room and exploration pages are no longer built or published.

The scene reuses the shared garden-square geometry with flat-ground, uniform-roofline, pinboard-wall, Cornwall Gardens, palette and furnishing settings. The entire London garden-square vignette, including its terraces, room and furnishings, is at 63% of its original scale: the earlier 20% and 25% reductions followed by a 5% increase. The Cornwall Gardens model has white façades, two buildings on each east/west side, and no benches or fountain. Its garden, paths, fences and side paving are shortened by one 4.44-unit building bay, keeping the rear boundary fixed; trees and lamps follow the shorter layout. Nine smaller neighbouring houses, including three behind the study, fill gaps outside the square using the same plain pastel blocks and flat grey roof caps as the rest of London, with no windows, doors, chimneys or trim. The square's interior and room approach remain open. Camera targets and object anchors follow the shared group transform.

The default city view starts 15 degrees south of west of the study and looks 15 degrees north of east at approximately 21 degrees above the horizon, keeping the study centred while revealing the city beyond it. Returning to London restores this starting viewpoint. Study and object close-ups retain their own camera angles.

## Geography and licence

The bundled [`geography.json`](./geography.json) contains 679 named public parks/gardens from [OS Open Greenspace](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/products/os-open-greenspace), 50 tidal-water features and three inland-water features from [OS OpenMap Local](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/products/os-open-map-local). These are genuine TQ shapefile polygons, not traced or substituted approximations. The JSON records source IDs/layers, anonymous download endpoints, archive checksums, retrieval date and processing parameters.

The Serpentine, Long Water and Round Pond use the reviewed `SurfaceWater_Area` IDs listed in the extractor. That layer has no name field, so these three names are editorial location labels, not OS-supplied names. The extractor fails if those IDs disappear. Trees are excluded from water polygons. Green parks/trees, blue water and varied pastel buildings belong to the map; the interface remains charcoal/chalk.

Contains Ordnance Survey data © Crown copyright and database right 2026. Reused under the [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/). Attribution is available through the page’s “OS geography & credits” control.

Coordinates remain British National Grid, **EPSG:27700**. The 26 × 20 km clip is `[516000, 170000, 542000, 190000]` metres, over five times the original prototype’s area. Rendering maps eastings to x and negative northings to z at 100 metres per scene unit. Heights and object scales are independently exaggerated.

The offline conversion simplifies rings at up to 5 metres, triangulates polygons with holes, checks triangulated area against ring area, clips each triangle to the rectangle, then quantises vertices to 0.1 metres. Simplification is reduced when that area check fails; refinements are explicitly recorded. Clipping triangles avoids joining disconnected polygon fragments across the clip boundary. The bundled result has 13,027 triangles and is approximately 513 KB before compression. Rendering merges it into two meshes.

## Reproduce the bundle

Run from the repository root with Node.js, Python 3, `curl` and `unzip`. `npm ci` restores the locked build dependencies, including the Three.js triangulator. No GIS service or API key is required.

```sh
npm ci
mkdir -p .cache/london-os/greenspace .cache/london-os/water
curl -fL 'https://api.os.uk/downloads/v1/products/OpenGreenspace/downloads?area=TQ&format=ESRI%C2%AE+Shapefile&redirect' -o .cache/london-os/greenspace.zip
curl -fL 'https://api.os.uk/downloads/v1/products/OpenMapLocal/downloads?area=TQ&format=ESRI%C2%AE+Shapefile&redirect' -o .cache/london-os/openmap.zip
unzip -jo .cache/london-os/greenspace.zip '*GreenspaceSite.*' -d .cache/london-os/greenspace
unzip -jo .cache/london-os/openmap.zip '*TidalWater.*' -d .cache/london-os/water
unzip -jo .cache/london-os/openmap.zip '*SurfaceWater_Area.*' -d .cache/london-os/water
curl -fL 'https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GeospatialPython/pyshp/2.3.1/shapefile.py' -o .cache/london-os/shapefile.py
PYTHONPATH=.cache/london-os python3 scripts/london-os-extract.py .cache/london-os > .cache/london-os/raw.json
node scripts/london-os-bundle.mjs
npm run build
npm test
```

The Python reader is unmodified, MIT-licensed [pyshp 2.3.1](https://github.com/GeospatialPython/pyshp/tree/2.3.1), used offline and not shipped. CP1252 decodes the source DBF names. Raw archives total approximately 225 MB and are not committed. Remove the specific `.cache/london-os` directory after processing.

The official download URLs track the current edition. The bundler verifies the original archive MD5 values recorded in its source before writing output. If OS replaces an archive, preserve the old zip for exact reproduction, or review the new [Greenspace metadata](https://api.os.uk/downloads/v1/products/OpenGreenspace/downloads?area=TQ&format=ESRI%C2%AE%20Shapefile) and [OpenMap Local metadata](https://api.os.uk/downloads/v1/products/OpenMapLocal/downloads?area=TQ&format=ESRI%C2%AE%20Shapefile), then deliberately update checksums and the retrieval date. Do not bypass the check. SHA-256 values for both original archives are also recorded in the bundle.

## Interaction and limitations

Drag or touch-drag to orbit; scroll or pinch to zoom. There are no separate zoom, rotation or reset buttons, and the floating destination buttons have no leader lines. One Explore button flies into the centred office. Its computer opens CV, shelves open Projects/Reading, the wall pinboard opens Work history and the cupboard opens Interests. Five individual lanyards/access cards reuse the original playful company illustrations. Each camera move stays in the same scene. Books open and passes turn over; the original readable content appears alongside them. Back, Escape, browser history and deep links work at every level. Reduced motion skips camera flights.

The Interests cabinet stands against the right wall with its front facing left into the room; its anchor and camera approach follow that orientation. The pinboard, frame and five lanyards are uniformly 28% smaller than the preceding version. Work history now approaches the board almost head-on at a close, device-specific distance, while selecting an individual pass still lifts and turns it in the room. Its top-right label is simply "Pinboard", without a duplicate panel subtitle. The masthead omits the location line. Reading buttons include title and author; the duplicate shelf headings/lists are omitted from this panel, while individual book links and the accessible source portfolio are retained.

The computer uses the shared terminal directly on its monitor, not in a side card. Three.js CSS3DRenderer matches the live HTML input to the monitor's world position, orientation and dimensions, including when orbiting. A CanvasTexture mirrors its output, draft input and command menu onto the physical monitor from the first frame, so the state remains visible when zoomed out and respects WebGL depth occlusion. The interactive HTML layer appears only in the computer close-up. The distant cursor is static; the focused HTML cursor blinks. Its close camera approach keeps the `justinattw$` prompt and clickable `\ for commands` legible. Command help exposes `download resume.pdf`, `open resume.pdf` (new tab), `cat education.txt`, `open linkedin` (new tab to the supplied profile) and `exit` (back to the study). Backslash or clicking help keeps the input focused; Up/Down highlight a command with wrapping and Enter or clicking pastes and runs it. The help button remains available after commands; Escape closes command help before leaving the computer. Education is read from the semantic portfolio, and the static CV remains available in the no-WebGL/no-JavaScript fallbacks. Other object cards fit their contents up to a viewport limit instead of stretching to the bottom; mobile camera framing updates when card height changes.

The desk computer is a simple, unbranded laptop with a very thin silver base, a flat black keyboard inset and a slim, backward-tilted black lid. The live screen retains the terminal above, including the distant state texture. Its camera target and viewing direction follow the actual screen transform. Destination labels are simply Computer, Project shelf, Reading shelf, Pinboard and Cabinet.

The study has a smaller rug, two rectangular white-framed windows with genuinely open wall apertures and lightly tinted transparent glazing, and no leftover roof fragment. Green wallpaper continues along both full side walls, with white cornices and skirting (the later green-wall request supersedes the earlier white-wall direction). A monstera and bamboo reuse the original plant models and disappear in object close-ups. The all-black cat reuses the original articulated model with a room-specific route: it grooms, drinks from its raised bowl, walks, jumps onto the chair and desk, paws at the keyboard and bats the monstera, which ruffles in response. Cat motion pauses in close-ups, in hidden tabs and for reduced motion. The compact London title panel is wider, with smaller type and no study invitation.

Back controls are grouped on the right, immediately left of the dark location label, including Back to shelf/passes for selected items. Both controls stretch to the same height and vertically centre their contents. LinkedIn and GitHub links live in the bottom bar instead of Work history, replacing the orbit/zoom hint and retaining outbound arrows. They open new tabs only after a click. Work history lists each company once in its picker; the selected company's name appears once as the panel heading. Project and reading shelf approaches are substantially closer and reading is centred between its two book rows. Object markers are hidden during close-ups so they cannot cover the monitor input.

On viewports up to 760px wide, Explore fits the room to the screen width with a small margin. Its distance follows the room bounds, camera angle and viewport aspect ratio instead of a fixed mobile zoom. Object close-ups hide the masthead and footer. Back/location controls move to the top edge and content cards towards the bottom edge, reclaiming the bars' space without hiding the way back. Both bars return in the study and city views; desktop navigation remains unchanged. Garden-square trees are twice their previous height, with their widths and positions unchanged. This does not resize trees in London's parks.

Controls follow actual camera-to-room distance, including manual wheel, pinch and button zoom. Explore switches immediately to the five fully positioned object buttons at 16 scene units, and back outside that distance. There is no opacity crossfade or distance-interpolated fan-out, so pausing zoom cannot leave a half-expanded control set. Zooming away closes an object’s reading panel without forcing another camera flight. Automatic city/office changes replace the hash rather than adding history entries.

The content plugin injects `src/portfolio.html` into the homepage before the project and reading transforms. It fails the build if the CV/work source contract changes, rather than silently shipping empty content. `View contents`, a skip link, WebGL-failure handling and a JavaScript-disabled document keep the content accessible in the same page.

The scene renders on demand outside the study, with animation frames during flights, object motion and the visible cat's routine. All office furnishings are created before the first frame, so zooming in manually reveals the same populated room. Geography, fonts and portfolio content are bundled locally: no runtime external requests, map SDKs, accounts or click-throughs to another portfolio study. Outbound links and the CV download remain explicit visitor actions.

Only the park and water footprints are OS geography. City blocks, street-like gaps, trees and all heights are illustrative. Royal Albert Hall, Big Ben, the London Eye, Buckingham Palace, [Kensington Palace](https://www.ukcitymap.com/kensington-palace-greater-london-gps-location-coordinates.html), [Albert Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Bridge,_London), Nelson's Column and Marble Arch are original toy silhouettes, not surveyed building models. Kensington Palace remains a single block without the previously modelled side wings, now with a hipped slate roof, capped chimneys, side windows and a detailed west entrance with sash windows and steps. Buckingham Palace has a courtyard, detailed principal façade, balcony, roofline and forecourt railings; its garden front adds sash windows, a projecting central bay, columned porch, pediment and terrace steps. These rear elevations are artistic interpretations, not measured architectural reconstructions. The Eye's supports rotate with its wheel. Marble Arch has three genuinely open passages; Nelson's Column includes a pedestal, capital, statue and four small lion silhouettes. Background buildings and trees avoid landmark clearances. The bridge’s pink/cream towers and cables are deliberately exaggerated. The enlarged terrace and open-roof workspace are roughly in South Kensington, not at a precise address. Its garden square is part of that illustrative vignette, not an additional OS greenspace polygon.

Landmark orientations were measured offline on 19 November 2026 from OpenStreetMap contributors' data: the [palace footprint](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/30089855) and [bridge centreline](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/1188809166) with its [southern continuation](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/591291859). The palace east-front wall segment runs from `(51.5052137, -0.1873780)` to `(51.5049053, -0.1872354)`; the bridge axis runs from `(51.4831672, -0.1671365)` to `(51.4813001, -0.1662055)` (latitude, longitude). Bearings use mean-latitude longitude scaling and an approximate 1.43-degree correction from true north to BNG grid north. The measured palace footprint normal is approximately 72.5 degrees; the bridge runs north-northwest to south-southeast (161.3 degrees southbound). Only rotations are derived from this data, not building geometry. [OpenStreetMap attribution and licence](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) are linked in the credits.

The [Eye footprint](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/204068874) gives its horizontal diameter a grid bearing of approximately 5.8 degrees (near north-south), measured between the short-edge midpoints `(51.50274025, -0.11980015)` and `(51.50394295, -0.11955285)`. [Buckingham Palace's frontage](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/348343214), from `(51.5016636, -0.1420925)` to `(51.5009548, -0.1413450)`, faces northeast at approximately 55.3 degrees. The same calculation gives [Marble Arch's central passage](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/667510421) a southbound bearing of 166.3 degrees. Approximate landmark locations remain unchanged except for the new monuments, positioned from Historic England's listed-building records: [Nelson's Column](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1276052), TQ 30017 80419 (`530017, 180419`), and [Marble Arch](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1239534), TQ 27852 80968 (`527852, 180968`). Contains Historic England information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0; no record text, photographs or protected map images are bundled.

Both palace models receive the user's explicitly requested 180-degree visual flip from the preceding version. Their y rotations are now `360 - bearing`, rather than `180 - bearing`; the actual model entrance/balcony vectors therefore have grid bearings 252.5 and 235.3 degrees. This is a user-directed visual orientation, not a new geographic measurement; the source normals above are retained as provenance. Their anchors remain BNG `(525844, 180027)` for [Kensington Palace](https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101223861-kensington-palace-campden-ward) and `(528997, 179614)` for [Buckingham Palace](https://britishlistedbuildings.co.uk/101239087-buckingham-palace-st-jamess-ward). These are location references, not surveyed model extents. Tests check the actual entrance/balcony vectors, not only the groups' local axes.

The eastern skyline adds original, exaggerated models of the Shard (split glass spire), Walkie Talkie (flared tower and roof garden), Gherkin (rounded taper and diagonal lattice), and nine Canary Wharf high-rises centred on a pyramid-topped One Canada Square. Approximate locations were estimated offline from the published coordinates for [the Shard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shard) (`51.5045, -0.0865`), [20 Fenchurch Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20_Fenchurch_Street) (`51.51128, -0.08353`), [30 St Mary Axe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_St_Mary_Axe) (`51.51444, -0.08028`) and [One Canada Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Canada_Square) (`51.50500, -0.019611`). A local tangent-plane estimate from the existing Big Ben anchor, with approximate grid convergence, was rounded to 50 metres: BNG `(532950, 180150)`, `(533100, 180900)`, `(533350, 181250)` and `(537550, 180300)` respectively. This is illustrative placement, not a surveyed coordinate conversion. The other Canary Wharf towers are an artistic cluster, not individual surveyed buildings. No source text, imagery or third-party models are bundled.

TidalWater omits some under-bridge areas; those source gaps remain visible. Only the three reviewed inland lakes are included. The selected greenspace class excludes private gardens and other land-use classes. Buildings are not a real street network, and their grid is not a substitute for OS building data. The garden-square terraces share a consistent roofline; background blocks still vary illustratively. Distance fog hides the world’s horizon rather than exposing a tabletop edge.

The shared Playwright configuration uses port 4182 and runs scene-heavy tests one worker at a time. Tests cover desktop mouse/mobile touch orbit, zoom-driven marker coalescence, centred composition, full-width masthead, initial furnishings/landmarks, the measured garden reduction, all five destinations, unobstructed physical passes, books, history/deep links, keyboard focus, reduced motion, no external requests, source geometry invariants, both fallbacks and the single published homepage. Screenshots are written under `test-results/`.

Royal Albert Hall is uniformly 5% smaller than its previous silhouette. Its original stylised geometry adds two tiers of arched glazing, cream mullions and pilasters, a decorative frieze and fine dome ribs.

The [Design Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_Museum) remains at 45% of its earlier linear size and is placed at BNG `(524700, 179550)`, on the southwest corner of the bundled Holland Park outline as requested. This is a deliberate illustrative corner placement, not its surveyed building centroid. Its low glazed pavilion and saddle-shaped roof are original geometry, not an imported architectural model. Nearby background blocks and trees leave it clear.

Headless Chromium phone screenshots can show an unresolved dark rectangle over close-ups, despite passing scene hit tests. Actual-device rendering has not been verified.
